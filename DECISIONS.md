## 2026-11-13 - M08B multi-good economy and the accepted 1000-seed baseline

The M08B multi-good economy and its long-run simulation rules were approved on 2026-11-12 and are recorded in [GDD_v4.0.md](GDD_v4.0.md) §14.2. This entry records the rules that are now authoritative, the current 1000-seed result, and the user's decision about that result.

- Goods and money: `market.food` and `market.materials`, with funds as the only currency; intelligence does not trade. Production materials keep using the existing industry supply stock, food has its own stock, both start at price index 1000‰, and the base price is 1 fund per unit.
- Household demand: each month charges `ceil(living family members at month start / 5)` food units at the previous settled food price. The purchase is the minimum of demand, available stock and affordable funds; no debt is created, and unmet units accumulate into the consecutive-shortage count.
- Prices: after each month, `pressure‰ = roundHalfUp((demand - available supply) * 1000 / max(demand, available supply, 1))`, `change‰ = clamp(pressure‰ / 4, -100, +100)`, and the new index is clamped to `100..100000‰`. The 50/50 food/materials basket is the composite index; a doubling year over year is extreme inflation.
- Shortage and mortality: each completed 3 consecutive shortage months adds 50‰ annual death probability at the next M08A year boundary, up to +250‰, and a fully supplied month resets the count. This is a separate addend to the approved age bands, not a replacement for them.
- Industry: production materials are withdrawn as before and additionally cost `ceil(units * materials index / 1000)` funds at the previously settled index. Farm self-retained supplies are priced too, and the farm additionally yields 4 food. Insufficient funds or supplies keep the existing stall semantics: no partial charge and no overdraft.
- Persistence: schema v17 adds the market block (food stock, the food and materials price indices, settled month index, consecutive shortage count, composite history). Production supplies stay in the existing industry block (`industry.supplies`), so there is only one supply stock. v16→v17 moves at most one month of food demand out of that old supply stock, keeps every other field, and starts both indices at 1000‰ and the shortage count at 0 without inventing famine history.
- Panel preview parity: `IndustryView.Build` now derives each slot status from a `IndustrySettlement.Plan` call on the same state, date and persisted materials index the month turn uses, so a slot cannot show 运作中 while settlement stalls on material cost. The settlement stays the single decision rule; the previous read-only check remains only as the fallback presentation when the planner rejects an inconsistent ledger. The already-settled branch is a defensive guard only: the save invariant keeps `LastSettledMonthIndex == MonthIndex(date) - 1` at every stable save, so a previewed month is never already settled, and the committed report is not required for normal previews. Regression: `IndustryPanelTests.IndustryPanel_PredictsStalledForMaterialCostShortfall` (funds 8, supplies 12, materials index 1500‰, assigned trading house) asserts the displayed status is Stalled and the real month close reports the same line status.
- Current result (2026-11-13, current rule set, 20/1/200/1000 staged reruns): 1000 seeds over 100 years end as 605 extinct and 395 stopped at a pending succession decision, with 0 completions. Year 10 reaches 194/1000, year 25 reaches 2/1000, and years 50 and 100 reach 0/1000. Cumulative food shortage, economic collapse and extreme inflation each report 999/1000; industry idling 0/1000, relationship anomalies 0/48 sampled seeds, and world-progress deadlock 0/1000.
- Acceptance: the user explicitly accepted the current result and its survival-balance risk. This acceptance does not make 50-year or 100-year survival a pass, does not claim the risk is fixed, and does not replace the remaining M08B sign-off items.
- Boundaries: the run is an unattended baseline that never appoints industry heads, so it is not a player-strategy success rate. Fifty- and hundred-year population, age and wealth denominators are zero and must not be read as zero values or as a pass. The GDD sets no economic balance threshold, so changing mortality, demand or price rules needs separate approval.
- Related repair in the same worktree: the migration error text now reports the real supported source range (`v1–v16`) when migrating to v17.

## 2026-11-13 - M08B Task 4 correction: event-offer applicability, choice order and heir-gap population

- Scope note: the correction follow-up for M08B Task 4 adds one player-facing event rule beyond the original runner work, so it is recorded here rather than only in code. The GDD's event design is unchanged; the rule removes an unplayable offer.
- Offer rule: `EventEngine` offers a monthly event only when at least one option is structurally applicable under the current head — its requirements are met and every `FamilyRelationshipDelta` target is a current, non-head family member. A context without a head leaves the check off; a head with an empty roster is a complete head-only family, so family effects are invalid and the scene is excluded. Value ranges, NPC ranges and doctrine state stay commit-time checks.
- Offer-set scope: the rule is set-wide, not SC-018-specific: it applies to every monthly scene whose options all carry family effects, whatever scenario tags the row. The SC-024 creed council rows (`event.sc024.creed-council.{secrecy,righteousness,merit}`) are inside that set because every one of their options applies `FamilyRelationshipDelta` to `person.sc013.masatsuna` and `person.sc013.nobumasa`; once either man is the head, no council option is applicable and the row is withheld. The two checks behave asymmetrically on purpose: succession-count conditions are commit-stable, evaluated in this offer pass only and never re-checked at commit, while family effect targets are revalidated at commit against the current head. Both passes share one head rule, so an offered option is never rejected later for the same reason.
- Rationale: SC-018 scenes address named family members. When succession makes one of those members the head, the ordered `(head, member)` pair the effect needs does not exist, so the option could only be rejected. Before this rule the scene was offered and every option failed at commit, which blocked the calendar; the audit found 61/1000 seeds stopped this way. The rule does not retarget an effect, drop a queued choice, or change a succession rule.
- Player effect: a scene is withheld only when every option addresses the current head; its rows stay in the catalog and it can still be offered in a generation where the target is not the head. Neutral events and scenes with one valid target are unchanged. `TryGetEventOptionAvailability` now runs the same preflight as commit, so an option is never shown as selectable and then rejected.
- Legacy saves: a save written before this correction can already hold the state the gate prevents — a scene queued while its target was a member, followed by a handoff to that target. There is no automatic repair, migration, retarget or forced choice for those saves: the loader only checks that a pending event exists in the active scenario, so the stuck scene stays queued with every option unavailable and the calendar blocked. The guard prevents generating that state in new play, and the pending-event order rule below stops a new handoff from creating it; an already-invalid save needs an explicit player decision, not a silent repair.
- Player order: while an event is queued, `ConfirmSuccession`, `BeginSuccessionRetirement`, `AcceptCrisisAdoption` and `CanBeginSuccessionRetirement` refuse with `SuccessionOutcome.BlockedByPendingEvent`. A queued scene resolves against the current head, and confirming the handoff first would make its options address the incoming head and strand the player; selecting a successor or regent stays allowed because it changes no head. The runner already resolves the queued event before succession, so both paths share one order.
- Heir-gap metric: `heirGapAtStop` numerator and denominator both describe stopped seeds whose family is not extinct and still has living members. An extinct family keeps its roster rows, so the earlier living-member denominator counted 14 extinct-during-succession seeds that the numerator correctly excludes; the 1000-seed aggregate now reads 395/395 instead of 395/409. `FinalFamilyExtinct` is persisted in the normalized seed row for that reason.
- Tests: `M08BLongTermSimulationTests` covers the sole-head/empty-roster exclusion, availability parity, the pending-event succession gate (confirm and retirement), the old-head choice before a handoff, and the heir-gap population with the extinct-with-living-members shape. `SC024M01RegressionTests.Sc024_CouncilRowIsWithheldOnlyWhenAFamilyTargetIsTheHead` pins the same offer gate on a real SC-024 council row: offered while the head is not one of its two effect targets, withheld as soon as either target becomes the head.

## 2026-11-12 - M08A annual demography inside the monthly transaction

User approved the M08A implementation plan and start on 2026-11-12; the approved rules are recorded in [GDD_v4.0.md](GDD_v4.0.md).

- Mortality: exactly one annual check at the December→January boundary. Age bands are evaluated per-mille (`0–14` 5, `15–39` 5, `40–59` 10, `60–69` 40, `70–79` 120, `80+` 250), using the age at the new date. The candidate snapshot is taken before the month's births and each still-living member consumes one WorldSimulation roll in ordinal `PersonId` order, so the same save and seed replay the same death set. A non-boundary month consumes no death roll.
- Ordering: due births/conceptions first (preserving the existing M04B stream usage), then annual deaths, then autonomous marriage rolls. A gestating parent who gives birth and dies in the same settlement gives birth first; a dead gestating parent's unfinished pregnancy is cancelled; newborns never enter that year's mortality list.
- Head death: exactly one existing `SuccessionService.BeginDeathSuccession` call. A pending succession decision from the month skips NPC marriage. With no lawful heir, the existing crisis/extinction decision still runs instead of the month being silently skipped.
- NPC marriage: only the annual boundary. Candidates are living family members other than the player-controlled current head, at or above `AdulthoodAge`, with a specified gender and no living formal spouse. Widowed people remain eligible, no fertility-age filter applies, each eligible person consumes one 15% roll, and the list is built once so a spouse generated this year cannot be proposed again in the same pass.
- Spouse identity: stable ID `person.marriage.<sanitized-candidate>.<year>` with a collision suffix, an original given name reused from the birth naming tables with the same collision rule, the opposite gender, and an adult birth year. The age offset is a stable hash in `[-5, +5]` clamped to adulthood, so marriages consume no extra random values.
- Transaction: plans are pure; mutations happen on a cloned succession snapshot. The death/marriage chronicle block is validated for the next append slots before the first write, and the test seam advances the ledger so the real append fails. Any failure restores date, succession (persons, life, members, kinship, pregnancies), development, daily actions, industry, RNG, chronicle and the other monthly state, and a retry consumes the same RNG order.
- Persistence: no new save field. Generated spouses use the existing person/life/member/growth/daily-action/kinship records; deaths use the existing `Alive` flag. Schema stays v16 and no migration branch changes.
- Chronicle/UI: `ChronicleCategory.NaturalDeath = 8` is append-only with source `natural-death` (existing enum values unchanged). The family chronicle panel renders `人物离世 · 享年 N 岁`, Campaign Review's People section reads the real death and marriage rows, and the existing family tree already renders the `Spouse` edge and `已故` label. No new panel, avatar or scene root was added.
- Scope guard: no package, scene, prefab, external asset, paid service, or M08B price-index/inflation work. The M08A slice is implementation-complete once the focused EditMode suites pass; Play Mode acceptance stays with the lead and the user.

## 2026-11-12 - M08B deterministic succession for long simulations

The user approved a deterministic succession policy for M08B long simulations. The policy runs only in the simulation adapter and uses existing `GameSession` succession APIs. It does not change player-facing succession.

- Choose the valid designated heir when one exists. Otherwise, choose the eligible heir with the lowest stable ID.
- Choose a regent from the legal candidates by lowest stable ID.
- Keep the existing stop classification when no legal heir exists. Do not drop extinction or unresolved-event seeds from the results.

## 2026-11-10 - M05 ships one hand-authored escort commission instead of a quest framework

- Decision: Implement M05 as a single hardcoded commission (`mission.shadow-escort`, 「护送委托」) that reuses the existing mission, daily-action, industry-resource, deterministic-random, save/migration and chronicle patterns. No quest DSL, mission catalogue, scripted condition system, new package, scene, prefab, or third-party framework is added. The offer, the assignee, the timed execution, the attribute check and the settled outcome all live in one plain C# class, so the slice stays reviewable.
- Lifecycle: `NotAccepted → Accepted → InProgress → Succeeded | PartialSuccess | Failed`. Accepting in 清洲 deducts `4` funds once (`2` under `家训：隐密至上`). Assigning takes exactly one living current-family adult who is not retired, has no active daily action, no industry post and a known location; those conflicts are rejected with a reason instead of running in parallel.
